It brings together books, essays, plays, and the Decision Integrity Protocol: literary and practical work concerned with truth, perception, memory, responsibility, and the conditions that help people move confidently toward consequential decisions.

The books and plays explore these questions through story, character, language, and lived experience. The essays consider them directly. 04 · Decision Integrity Protocol

Decision Integrity Protocol

A structured method for examining consequential decisions.

The Decision Integrity Protocol is designed for circumstances in which certainty is unavailable, but action cannot simply be postponed.

Important decisions are rarely shaped by facts alone. They are also shaped by pressure, fear, loyalty, desire, habit, institutional incentives, incomplete information, personal history, competing obligations, and the narratives people rely on when they need to make sense of a situation.

The protocol provides a disciplined way to examine those conditions before they harden into a decision.

It asks questions such as:

  • What decision is actually being made?
  • What is known, and what is being assumed?
  • What information is missing, uncertain, or being discounted?
  • What pressures, loyalties, incentives, fears, or habits may be influencing judgment?
  • Who will bear the consequences of action, inaction, delay, or error?
  • What values are being protected, compromised, or left unspoken?
  • What evidence or circumstance would require the decision to be reconsidered?
  • Can the reasoning behind this choice be explained honestly to the people affected by it?

The protocol does not promise certainty, perfect outcomes, or a decision free from consequence. A careful decision can still lead to an unwanted result; an irresponsible one can occasionally be rewarded by luck.

Its purpose is to strengthen the quality of judgment itself: to help ensure that a decision is informed, proportionate to the stakes, open to correction, and accountable to those it affects.

Taught as five capabilities:

  • Question FidelityAnswer the question that was actually asked.
  • Proposition IntegrityTell observation, report, inference and finding apart.
  • Qualifier ProtectionKeep the conditions that make a claim true.
  • Authority MappingSeparate access from the authority to act.
  • Repair ProtocolFix the first break, then trace what depends on it.

The Keeping Room

The Keeping Room

A monthly essay from Wheeler Hubbell Publishing.

The Keeping Room is the monthly editorial publication of Wheeler Hubbell Publishing.

Each month, Wheeler Hubbell publishes a considered piece of writing on books, plays, art, memory, truth, language, publishing, decision-making, or another question arising from the work.

Some pieces will develop ideas from the books or the Decision Integrity Protocol. Some will be fragments, notes, observations, or finished essays that belong nowhere else. The purpose is not to manufacture content or maintain a cycle of announcements. It is to publish work when there is something worth saying.

One essay each month. No promotional clutter.

About

About Wheeler Hubbell

Wheeler Hubbell is a writer, author, playwright, publisher, and decision-integrity consultant.

His work spans literary writing, dramatic writing, editorial development, independent publishing, and the development of the Decision Integrity Protocol. Across these forms, he is interested in the relationship between language and perception: how people describe what they believe, what they omit, what they cannot yet know, and how those descriptions shape the decisions they make.

He works with individuals and organizations facing consequential choices in conditions of uncertainty, competing priorities, incomplete information, or lasting consequence.

Wheeler Hubbell Publishing is the home for this work: books, essays, plays, and a practical method for approaching difficult decisions with greater clarity and accountability.
