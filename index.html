SECTION III The Method · Eight Inquiries · Bounded Judgment

Decision Integrity Protocol

A structured method for examining consequential decisions when certainty is unavailable and action cannot simply be postponed.

Separating Assertion from Authority

Consequential failures rarely occur because of raw ignorance. They occur when an unverified assertion is quietly granted the weight of authoritative fact, or when an institution answers a convenient substitute question in place of the real one.

The Decision Integrity Protocol provides a disciplined, bounded sequence to examine conditions before they harden into irrevocable consequence. It does not promise immunity from error; it guarantees that a decision is proportioned to its stakes and accountable to the evidence.

I. Question Fidelity: Preserving the exact consequential inquiry against convenient substitution.
II. Proposition Integrity: Refusing to accept proof of an adjacent premise as proof of the whole.
III. Qualifier Protection: Preventing probabilistic estimates from being cited as categorical certainty.
IV. Authority Mapping: Tracing assertions to their primary evidentiary origin.
V. Repair Protocol: Defining explicit conditions that require immediate reconsideration.

THE EIGHT FUNDAMENTAL INQUIRIES
  1. What decision is actually being made? Distinguishing the true consequential choice from cosmetic procedure.
  2. What is known, and what is being assumed? Isolating verified primary records from contextual extrapolation.
  3. What information is missing, uncertain, or being discounted? Exposing inconvenient gaps that have been smoothed over.
  4. What pressures or loyalties may be influencing judgment? Identifying institutional incentives, fear of delay, and habit.
  5. Who will bear the consequences of action, delay, or error? Mapping the human distribution of consequence.
  6. What values are being protected, compromised, or left unspoken? Surfacing the unwritten trade-offs governing the choice.
  7. What evidence would require the decision to be reconsidered? Establishing falsification criteria prior to execution.
  8. Can the reasoning be explained honestly to those affected? Subjecting the justification to the scrutiny of those bearing the cost.

The Crisis of Unsupervised Agentic Drift

As autonomous artificial intelligence agents transition from conversational interfaces to consequential enterprise and legal workflows, organizations face an unprecedented failure mode: epistemic drift. When an agent experiences probabilistic hallucination or unauthorized context switching, downstream systems execute actions based on unverified premises.

The Authority Conservation Principle

At the heart of the release is Wheeler Hubbell's Authority Conservation Principle:

A(c) ≤ P(c)

The authority of any conclusion cannot exceed the verified provenance of its underlying record. If provenance is severed, downstream authority drops to zero.

The Mata v. Avianca Epistemic Autopsy

WHP's research is grounded in its landmark forensic analysis of Mata v. Avianca, where federal attorneys submitted synthetic judicial citations generated by language models. The WHP dossier traces the failure not to malicious intent, but to the absence of a deterministic boundary gate separating machine assertion from verified institutional record.
