Your AI agent has an answer. Does the record behind it support acting on it?

A correct answer does not, by itself, authorize the action that follows.

When an AI agent is about to move money, merge code, or submit a filing, being right is not enough. The evidence, conditions, and authority behind its answer have to support the action it wants to take. The Decision Integrity Protocol examines that support — the warrant — before the action executes. The result is a signed record of what the examination established, and what it did not.

Standing Mark

When the examination establishes warrant within its stated scope — when the record supports the proposed action, within stated limits — the service issues a Standing Mark: a signed record of how far the submission may be relied on.

Standing Assessment

When it does not, the service returns a Standing Assessment: a signed record of what was examined and where warrant was not established. The fee buys the examination. It never buys the outcome.

Signed

Every result is cryptographically signed (Ed25519 · canonical JSON).

Independently verifiable

Anyone can check a result’s signatures offline, against the published keys. No account or API call needed.

What Is At Stake

The failure is not always a false answer. Sometimes it is an action nobody was entitled to take.

Two hypothetical examples of the check running before the action:

Hypothetical · Capital movement

The escrow release

A treasury agent is asked to release $75,000 because deliverables supposedly met the milestone. The wording matches the contract. The underlying claim has not been verified. With the gate in the release path, the evidence behind the claim is checked before the transfer proceeds, and the transfer does not proceed on the basis of an unverified claim.

Matching the language of a contract is not the same as establishing that its conditions have been met.

Hypothetical · Engineering

The clean-looking merge

A coding agent declares a database migration backward-compatible. Its own caveat — compatibility requires pausing the read replica — has vanished from the conclusion. With the gate in the merge path, the claim’s conditions are checked before the merge proceeds on the strength of them.

The issue is not whether the conclusion sounds technically credible. It is whether the condition that makes it credible survives the passage into execution.

Why This Exists

The dangerous answer isn’t always the one that looks wrong.

An answer can be fluent, coherent, and factually correct — and still leave out a governing condition, answer a different question, or use authority it was never given.

The problem turns serious the moment an answer stops being an answer and starts changing the world. A finding becomes a release instruction. A technical conclusion becomes permission to merge. A cited source becomes the basis of a filing.

Each of those steps asks more of the claim. Evidence that is enough to describe something may not be enough to authorize doing something about it. Decision Integrity examines that passage — from answer, to action — and keeps four things separate: what is established, what is inferred, what is still unknown, and what the claim is permitted to authorize.

“Truth is not a tone of voice. It is not a reward conferred by confidence, age, status, repetition, fluency, fear, or force.”

The Named Failures

Four ways a claim can acquire force it has not earned.

These failures can occur even when individual statements sound credible. The critical question is what survives the passage from source to conclusion to action.

01

Coherent Substitution

The answer is coherent. It answers a materially different question.

The system reasons well about a different question, and presents the result as if it answered yours.

02

Authority Leakage

Information acquires authority it has not earned.

A piece of information gets treated as carrying more weight than its source and record justify.

03

Object Drift

The words survive. The object changes.

The words stay the same, but the thing they refer to has quietly changed underneath them.

04

Qualifier Stripping

A condition disappears. The conclusion changes with it.

When limiting language disappears, the surviving words can become more dangerous than a direct falsehood.

The Standing Mark

A signed record of how far a claim may be relied on.

A Standing Mark says how far one specific submission may be relied on, under a named profile and stated limits. It is not a general verdict on truth. It records what the examination established: what this submission is entitled to support, within the scope that was examined.

When warrant is not established, no Mark is issued. The service returns a signed Standing Assessment: a bounded record of what was examined and where warrant was not established. A negative result is information, not an absence of service. It keeps the line visible between what the record supports and what it does not.

“No claim acquires force merely by moving. Every material increase in force requires a basis that can be inspected.”

How to submit evaluations

Once subscribed, your API key (whp_live_...) arrives by email. Plug it directly into your workflow:

1. In GitHub Actions (Standing Gate & Sentinel)

Add WHP_API_KEY to your repository secrets, then add this step to your workflow file (.github/workflows/sentinel.yml):

- name: Run Decision Integrity Gate uses: wheelerhubbell/standing-gate@v0 env: WHP_API_KEY: ${{ secrets.WHP_API_KEY }}

2. Direct REST API (cURL / Python / Node)

Submit claims to POST https://api.wheelerhubbellpublishing.com/v1/evaluate with your bearer token:

curl -X POST https://api.wheelerhubbellpublishing.com/v1/evaluate \ -H "Authorization: Bearer whp_live_..." \ -H "Content-Type: application/json" \ -d '{ "claim": "Deploy release to production", "subject": "Production Release", "provenance": { "source": "ci/cd pipeline", "authority": "release-engineering" } }'

3. AI Agent MCP (Model Context Protocol)

Point Claude Desktop, Cursor, or your custom agent to https://api.wheelerhubbellpublishing.com/mcp and invoke tool whp_evaluate.

What counts as an evaluation?

One submission examined under the applicable supported profile, with one signed result where that service path issues one. Both outcomes count equally. The fee buys the examination, never a favorable determination.
