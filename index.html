What It Does

An examination before execution. A signed record after.

01

Submit the claim

Submit the proposed action and the record behind it: the evidence, qualifications, conditions, and authority on which it depends. The question is whether that record supports the transition from an answer to an action.

02

Examine the warrant

The service examines the submission against a named, published profile — a defined scope that states what the examination covers. The resulting record identifies what was checked, what was established, and the limits of the examination.

03

Receive the result

When warrant is established within the stated scope, the service issues a signed Standing Mark. When it is not, the service returns a signed Standing Assessment recording what was examined and where warrant was not established. The fee buys the examination. It never buys the outcome.

What Is At Stake

The failure is not always a false answer. Sometimes it is an unauthorized transition.

These hypothetical examples show how a workflow can use the examination before carrying out an action.

Hypothetical · Capital movement

The escrow release

A treasury agent receives a request to release $75,000 because deliverables have supposedly met milestone specifications. The wording matches the contract. The underlying claim has not been verified. If the gate is integrated into the release path, the evidence behind the claim is examined before the transfer proceeds. An unwarranted transition does not proceed on that basis.

Matching the language of a contract is not the same as establishing that its conditions have been met.
Hypothetical · Engineering

The clean-looking merge

A coding agent declares a database migration backward-compatible. Its own qualification — that compatibility requires a read-replica pause — has disappeared from the conclusion. If the gate is integrated into the merge path, the claim's conditions are examined before the merge proceeds on that basis.

The issue is not whether the conclusion sounds technically credible. It is whether the condition that makes it credible survives the passage into execution.
Why This Exists

The dangerous answer isn't always the one that looks wrong.

An answer can be fluent, coherent, and factually correct — and still omit a governing condition, answer a different question, or exercise authority it was never given.

The problem becomes consequential when an answer stops being an answer and starts changing the world. A finding becomes a release instruction. A technical conclusion becomes permission to merge. A cited source becomes the basis of a filing.

Each transition asks something new of the claim. Evidence sufficient to support a description may not be sufficient to authorize an action. Decision Integrity examines that passage. It distinguishes what is established from what is inferred, what is supported from what remains unknown, and what a claim may establish from what it is permitted to authorize.

“Truth is not a tone of voice. It is not a reward conferred by confidence, age, status, repetition, fluency, fear, or force.”Wheeler Hubbell · Decision Integrity: The Continuous Work · Preamble — The Difference Between a Word and Its Force
The Named Failures

Four ways a claim can acquire force it has not earned.

These failures can occur even when individual statements sound credible. The critical question is what survives the passage from source to conclusion to action.

01
Coherent Substitution
The answer is coherent. It answers a materially different question.

Reasoning well about a materially different proposition and presenting the result as responsive.

02
Authority Leakage
Information acquires authority it has not earned.

An informational object or relation acquires more force than its record entitles it to exercise.

03
Object Drift
The words survive. The object changes.

The language remains stable while the thing being reasoned about changes.

04
Qualifier Stripping
A condition disappears. The conclusion changes with it.

When limiting language disappears, the surviving words can become more dangerous than a direct falsehood.

The Standing Mark

A signed record of how far a claim may be relied on.

A Standing Mark states how far a submission may be relied on under a named profile and stated limits. It is not a universal verdict on truth. It records the standing established by the examination: what the submission is entitled to support within the scope examined.

When warrant is not established, no Mark is issued. The service returns a signed Standing Assessment — a bounded record of what was examined and where warrant was not established. A negative result is information, not an absence of service. It preserves the boundary between what the record supports and what remains unwarranted.

How to submit evaluations

Once subscribed, you receive your sovereign API key (whp_live_...) via email. Plug it directly into your workflow:

1. In GitHub Actions (Standing Gate & Sentinel)

Add WHP_API_KEY to your repository secrets, then add this step to your workflow file (.github/workflows/sentinel.yml):

- name: Run Decision Integrity Gate
  uses: wheelerhubbell/standing-gate@v0
  env:
    WHP_API_KEY: ${{ secrets.WHP_API_KEY }}
2. Direct REST API (cURL / Python / Node)

Submit claims to POST https://api.wheelerhubbellpublishing.com/v1/evaluate with your bearer token:

curl -X POST https://api.wheelerhubbellpublishing.com/v1/evaluate   -H "Authorization: Bearer whp_live_..."   -H "Content-Type: application/json"   -d '{
    "claim": "Deploy release to production",
    "subject": "Production Release",

  }'
