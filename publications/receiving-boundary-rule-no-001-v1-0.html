THE RECEIVING BOUNDARY RULE

Examination as a Condition of Consequential Acceptance

Wheeler Hubbell Publishing, Inc.

Rule No. 001

Version 1.0

Status: Public Adoption Instrument

⸻

PREAMBLE

A receiving system is not a passive endpoint.

It exercises control over what it recognizes, accepts, records, and permits to proceed.

Where a receiving party allows an unexamined proposition to become the basis of a consequential operation, the receiving party assumes the risk of treating assertion as sufficient evidence.

The Receiving Boundary Rule provides a mechanism for placing examination before consequential acceptance.

It allows an institution to establish, within its own authority, that designated propositions must satisfy a Decision Integrity examination requirement before they may be relied upon for specified operations.

The Rule does not require universal adoption.

It provides the receiving party with the instrument by which it establishes its own requirement.

1. THE RULE

A receiving party adopting this Rule establishes that:

A designated consequential proposition shall not be accepted as a sufficient evidentiary basis for a governed operation until the applicable Decision Integrity examination requirement has been satisfied and the resulting Standing Mark has been independently verified within its defined scope.

The requirement applies only to the classes of propositions and operations expressly designated by the receiving party.

The Rule does not presume that every communication, claim, or instruction requires examination.

2. RECEIVING PARTY AUTHORITY

The adopting receiving party retains control over:

The operations governed by the Rule.

The propositions requiring examination.

The applicable examination requirements.

The authority required for acceptance and execution.

The treatment of exceptions.

The consequences of failed or incomplete verification.

Adoption does not transfer these responsibilities to Wheeler Hubbell Publishing, Inc.

3. ADOPTION REQUIREMENTS

An adopting party must identify the following within its own authoritative operating instrument:

3.1 Designated operations

The consequential operations subject to examination.

3.2 Covered propositions

The claims, instructions, representations, or proposed transitions requiring examination.

3.3 Governing standard

The applicable version of the Decision Integrity Standard.

3.4 Required examination

The examination evidence and outcome required before acceptance.

3.5 Verification

The procedure by which the receiving party independently verifies the Standing Mark.

3.6 Decision authority

The actor or system authorized to determine whether the verified examination satisfies the receiving party’s requirements.

3.7 Exception authority

The authority permitted to approve an exception and the evidence required to establish it.

4. THE RECEIVING SEQUENCE

For governed operations, the receiving party must preserve the following sequence:

Reception: A proposition or instruction is received.

Identification: The receiving system identifies whether the proposition falls within a designated examination category.

Examination: The applicable proposition is submitted for examination.

Verification: The resulting Standing Mark is independently verified.

Acceptance decision: The receiving party determines whether the examination satisfies its requirements.

Authorization: The receiving party establishes the authority and conditions governing the proposed operation.

Execution: The authorized operation is performed.

Reconciliation: The resulting state is established independently of the preceding assertion.

These are distinct events.

Evidence of one event must not be substituted for evidence of another.

5. FAILURE HANDLING

Where the Rule applies, the receiving party must not treat an absent or insufficient examination record as satisfying the examination requirement.

The following conditions must remain distinguishable:

No examination record presented.

Examination record presented but unverifiable.

Examination record verified but outside the required scope.

Examination record verified but insufficient under the receiving party’s policy.

Examination requirement satisfied.

Acceptance decision made.

Operation authorized.

Operation executed.

Failure to satisfy the examination requirement does not independently establish that the proposed action is prohibited.

It establishes that the designated prerequisite has not been satisfied.

The receiving party must apply its own authorized failure-handling policy.

6. NO IMPLIED AUTHORIZATION

A positive Standing Mark does not itself authorize execution.

A receiving party must independently establish that the actor, instruction, and proposed operation possess the authority required by the receiver’s own governance.

The examination requirement and the authorization requirement are separate.

Satisfying one does not automatically satisfy the other.

7. EXCEPTIONS

An adopting party may establish an exception process through its own authorized governance.

Each exception must identify:

The operation or proposition affected.

The authority approving the exception.

The basis for the exception.

The scope and duration of its effect.

The evidence establishing that the exception was authorized.

An exception must not be represented as a successful Standing examination.

The receiving party must preserve the distinction between an operation proceeding under an exception and an operation proceeding after successful examination.

8. ADOPTION AND EFFECT

A receiving party adopts this Rule by incorporating it into an authoritative policy, operating contract, system requirement, agent instruction, or equivalent governance instrument under its control.

Adoption takes effect within the scope established by that instrument.

The receiving party may communicate its adoption to relevant counterparties and systems.

No directory listing, registry entry, commercial relationship, or separate recognition by Wheeler Hubbell Publishing, Inc. is required to make the receiving party’s own policy operative.

Adoption does not guarantee that an examination will result in a positive Mark or that a proposition will ultimately be accepted.

9. RELATIONSHIP WITH THE EXAMINATION INSTITUTION

Wheeler Hubbell Publishing, Inc. operates the Standing examination service under the Decision Integrity Standard and its applicable examination protocol.

The institution examines and records.

The receiving party establishes its requirements and decides whether to rely.

The receiving party’s adoption of this Rule does not give Wheeler Hubbell Publishing, Inc. authority over the receiving party’s subsequent decisions.

Likewise, the receiving party’s reliance on a Standing Mark does not give it authority to alter the examination record.

10. CONTINUITY OF RESPONSIBILITY

Adoption of the Receiving Boundary Rule establishes a continuing obligation within the receiver’s designated operations.

The receiving party must preserve answerability between the proposition received, the examination required, the record verified, the authority exercised, and the resulting state.

A change in representation does not independently establish a change in authority.

A change in reception does not independently establish a change in the underlying proposition.

A successful execution does not retrospectively establish that the required examination or authorization occurred.

11. FINAL RULE

The receiving party determines what it accepts.

The examination institution determines what it examines and records.

The authority governing the operation determines whether it may proceed.

The resulting state must establish what actually occurred.

No consequential operation should inherit authority merely from the assertion that preceded it.
