Answerability Under Transformation

Version 1.0

Status: Public Standard

PREAMBLE

The increasing delegation of consequential operations to autonomous agents and machine systems creates a problem that cannot be resolved by accuracy alone.

A system may produce a plausible answer without possessing authority to act upon it. A receiving system may accept an assertion without establishing its evidentiary basis. A transaction may execute successfully without establishing that the transition was authorized.

The integrity of a decision must therefore survive the movement from assertion to evidence, from evidence to authority, and from authority to consequence.

The Decision Integrity Standard establishes the requirements for examining that continuity.

It defines the relationship between a proposition, its evidentiary basis, the authority under which it is advanced, the examination performed upon it, and the consequential operation that may follow.

The governing principle is:

No conclusion may exceed what the record is entitled to establish.

1. OBJECTIVE

The Decision Integrity Standard establishes a common examination framework for consequential machine operations.

Its objectives are to:

Preserve the distinction between information and authority.

Establish answerability between assertions and their supporting evidence.

Identify discontinuities introduced through representation, transmission, acceptance, or execution.

Establish the scope and evidentiary basis of an examination.

Produce an independently verifiable examination record.

Preserve the receiving party’s responsibility for its own decisions.

The Standard applies wherever a proposition, instruction, representation, or proposed transition may become the basis for a consequential decision.

2. DEFINITIONS

2.1 Proposition

A defined assertion, claim, instruction, or proposed state transition submitted for examination.

2.2 Evidence

Information or records offered in support of a proposition or its asserted authority.

2.3 Authority

The valid basis under which an actor may make an assertion, exercise a right, issue an instruction, or cause a defined state transition.

2.4 Examination

A bounded evaluation of a submitted proposition against the applicable Decision Integrity requirements and the evidence within the examination’s defined scope.

2.5 Examination Record

The record identifying the examination performed, its scope, its outcome, and the information necessary to establish its provenance and verify its integrity.

2.6 Standing Mark

The signed examination record issued by the Standing service operated by Wheeler Hubbell Publishing, Inc.

2.7 Receiving Party

The independent actor, institution, agent, or system that receives a proposition or examination record and determines whether to rely upon it.

2.8 State Transition

A change in the recognized condition, status, ownership, authority, obligation, or other defined state of an object.

3. THE EXAMINATION REQUIREMENT

An examination must identify what is being examined and the limits of what the examination can establish.

The examination must not substitute an assertion for its evidence or infer authority merely from the presence of an instruction.

The examination must preserve the distinction between:

What is asserted.

What is evidenced.

What authority is established.

What is within the examination’s scope.

What the examination determines.

What remains for the receiving party to decide.

An examination is bounded by its submitted proposition, applicable protocol, available evidence, and recorded scope.

No examination outcome may be interpreted as establishing more than those elements support.

4. DECISION INTEGRITY

Decision Integrity requires continuity of answerability across the operations that transform a proposition into a consequential result.

The following distinctions are mandatory.

4.1 Representation is not identity.

A change in format, encoding, presentation, or representation does not independently establish a change in the underlying object.

4.2 Reception is not mutation.

Acceptance, rejection, interpretation, reliance, or misunderstanding by a receiving party does not independently alter the object received.

4.3 Assertion is not evidence.

A statement does not establish the truth, authority, or validity of its own contents merely by being made.

4.4 Examination is not authorization.

An examination record does not independently confer permission to execute the proposed action.

4.5 Acceptance is not execution.

Recognition or acceptance of a proposition is distinct from the subsequent operation that changes a state.

4.6 Execution is not retrospective validation.

Successful completion of an operation does not independently establish the validity of its preceding authorization.

These distinctions must remain observable throughout the decision process.

5. THE STANDING MARK

The Standing Mark is the examination instrument issued by Wheeler Hubbell Publishing, Inc. through its Standing service.

A positive Standing Mark represents a signed, scope-bound examination result.

Its purpose is to provide a receiving party with an independently verifiable record of examination that may satisfy an evidence requirement established by that receiving party.

The Mark is not a general warranty of truth, unrestricted certification, authorization, or guarantee of future performance.

Its evidentiary significance is bounded by the examination it records.

A receiving party must not interpret a positive Mark as granting authority that the underlying actor does not possess.

6. VERIFICATION AND RELIANCE

A receiving party must be able to distinguish an authentic examination record from an unsupported representation that an examination occurred.

Verification must be performed through the issuer’s applicable verification mechanism.

A receiving party must establish that the record presented is authentic, intact, attributable to the issuing authority, and applicable to the proposition for which reliance is proposed.

The receiving party must also determine whether the examination remains relevant to the operation under consideration.

The existence of a valid examination record does not compel reliance.

The receiving party remains responsible for determining whether the record satisfies its own evidentiary and operational requirements.

7. NEGATIVE, ABSENT, AND INDETERMINATE RESULTS

The absence of a positive Standing Mark means that the applicable Mark requirement has not been satisfied.

It does not, by itself, establish that the underlying action is prohibited or that the underlying proposition is necessarily false.

An invalid, missing, expired, unverifiable, or out-of-scope record must not be represented as a successful examination.

The receiving party must preserve the distinction between failure to establish a required condition and affirmative establishment of the opposite condition.

Uncertainty must not be silently converted into authorization.

8. STATE TRANSITION INTEGRITY

Where an examined proposition proposes a consequential state transition, the examination must not collapse the proposed transition into its asserted result.

The receiving party remains responsible for determining:

Whether the actor possesses the required authority.

Whether the applicable conditions have been satisfied.

Whether the proposed transition is permitted.

Whether the receiving system is authorized to execute it.

Whether the resulting state can be independently established.

The evidence of a completed transition must remain distinguishable from the evidence supporting its authorization.

9. INDEPENDENT RESPONSIBILITY

The examination institution and the receiving party exercise distinct responsibilities.

Wheeler Hubbell Publishing, Inc. operates the examination service and issues examination records within the defined scope of its Standard and applicable protocol.

The receiving party determines whether to accept, rely upon, or act on the examined proposition.

Neither responsibility substitutes for the other.

The examination institution does not assume the receiving party’s operational authority merely by issuing a Mark.

The receiving party does not acquire authority over the examination institution’s findings merely by receiving a Mark.

10. INSTITUTIONAL APPLICATION

This Standard is designed for independent reference and adoption.

It does not depend upon any particular directory, registry, marketplace, agent framework, or discovery provider.

A receiving institution may incorporate the Standard into its own governance, transaction, agent, or operating requirements.

The Standard defines the examination relationship. The receiving institution determines where that relationship is required within its own operations.

11. LIMITATIONS

Nothing in this Standard independently:

Creates ownership or transfers property.

Establishes an obligation where none otherwise exists.

Confers legal, financial, or operational authority.

Replaces consent, authorization, or applicable governance.

Guarantees the truth of every underlying assertion.

Guarantees subsequent conduct or performance.

Compels a receiving party to accept an examined proposition.

Transfers responsibility for a consequential decision to the examination institution.

12. GOVERNING PRINCIPLE

Decision Integrity is the continuous work of preserving answerability under transformation.

The examination establishes what was examined and what the examination determined.

The record establishes what can be verified about that examination.

The receiving party determines what it will accept.

The authority governing an operation determines whether it may proceed.

The resulting transition must establish what actually changed.

An answer is not an authorization. An examination is not a decision. A completed operation is not proof of the authority that preceded it.
